	Compare between without and with patch inference:
		A few hours later…

		without:
			For the moving video there isn’t much of a problem, as the cars and trucks are large enough to be detected. Occasionally, a car or a truck is not detected...
			For the static video, however, more than half are missing. The video is taken from too high up, which makes the cars too small for the algorithm to detect them.
	
		with:
			For the moving video, applying patching makes it possible to detect the few cars that were previously missing, but it becomes harder to detect trucks. The same trucks as before are still not detected, so I wonder if this issue comes directly from the algorithm rather than from the image. Also, sometimes duplicates are created on the same frame if too much patching is applied, and if patching is increased even further, detection precision starts to decrease
			For the static video, however, patching is essential. Thanks to it, up to four times more vehicles can be detected 

		So, for the moving video, patching is not necessarily required; I think a good parameter search may be sufficient, or perhaps just light patching. For the static video, however, patching is important, but the optimized parameters still need to be defined (number of patches, scaling, model)
